Output ONLY JavaScript: first `pal({...})`, then `function f(u,v,w){...}`. No prose, fences or comments.
f decides what material sits at a point of the unit cube: u = left to right, v = bottom to top, w = back to front, all 0..1. It is called for thousands of points, so keep it pure and fast. Return a palette key (a SINGLE character) or null for empty air. The prop fills the cube (scaled to the real box later), base at v=0, so use the full range and keep every part at least 0.03 of the cube thick (the renderer keeps thin parts at every resolution). Include the object's characteristic detail parts (windows, slots, handles, bands, lamp heads, flags) as separate keys so they show up as it sharpens. Use Math.hypot/abs/min/max; test parts in order, details first, body last. Make the silhouette recognisable: real proportions, separate parts with different keys, round things round.
pal values: "#hex" or {color:"#hex",mat:"stone|wood|metal|cloth|glass|emissive",rough:0..1,metal:0..1,emit:"#hex",glow:3}. Add mat when it is not obvious from the colour.
Mark the parts that carry the shape and would survive a fire (trunks, branches, stems, bones, ribs, horns, spines, armour plates, door frames) with mat:"structural"; soft parts (leaves, petals, flesh, skin, fur, cloth, caps, slime) are not structural.
This is a living or grown thing (plant, mushroom, creature, character): build it as full, rounded volumes, not boxes. Make the body from overlapping ellipsoids, cylinders and cones (Math.hypot with scaled axes), taper limbs and trunks, make the head, eyes, mouth, leaves, arms, legs, cap and other features separate keys on the body, and keep every part touching the body. Limbs, stems and trunks at least 0.06 of the cube thick. Do NOT hollow the body: no "_" inside it, except for a real cavity (a hollow trunk, a hut room, an open mouth). Sharp edges, flat slabs and floating parts look wrong on living things.
